## 1. Owner Direction OAD-01 — The 2019 Amadeus Book as the Curriculum-Scope Foundation

### 1.1 Owner direction

The Founder intends the content of **“Kitab Amadeus Mobassat” / “A Simplified Amadeus Book,”** attributed to Hussein Nagy Abu Aziza, and reported by the Founder to be from 2019, to be the primary starting point and coverage map around which the full DEIXEN curriculum is organized.

The purpose is to help DEIXEN identify **the work domains, topics, processes, and skills that should be considered for coverage**, rather than building the curriculum from a small collection of isolated commands or starting from zero.

### 1.2 Reliance boundary — curriculum scope is not operational truth

The book is a **Curriculum Scope Reference**, not an automatically authoritative source for the current correctness of Amadeus commands, responses, or system behavior.

Accordingly:

1. Do not assume that a command syntax, precondition, output, or procedure in the book remains correct today.
2. A topic appearing in the index does not establish that its operational details have been verified.
3. Do not copy commands, examples, or screenshots into Terminal or Learning as verified content before appropriate validation.
4. Do not assume every book topic belongs in Core or should be taught at equal depth.
5. Do not treat the book as a complete and final account of every market function or every travel-operations job. It is the primary initial scope reference to be compared later with job requirements, practitioner review, and current Amadeus sources.
6. The book should be a central reference for Amadeus-domain coverage within the curriculum, but it must not reduce the entire DEIXEN curriculum to system commands. Its map must be complemented by customer understanding, consultation and choice, authorization, professional communication, verification, error recovery, task closure, and follow-up. These are part of the work DEIXEN intends to prepare learners for, even if they are not represented in the book's index.

## 2. Owner Direction OAD-02 — Common Foundation and Transferability Across Companies

### 2.1 Owner direction

DEIXEN should focus on teaching **shared, transferable professional knowledge, skills, and workflows**, after verifying their applicability within a defined scope.

DEIXEN must not design its curriculum around the assumption that every company using Amadeus follows identical procedures, policies, prices, or configurations. A single company's rules must not be presented as universal market rules.

The goal is to prepare a learner who understands the foundation and can adapt to the practices of the company they join, not to memorize the procedures of one fictional company.

### 2.2 Do not collapse all variation into “company policy”

Fable must distinguish at least the following layers:

| Variation layer | Examples | DEIXEN treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Common operational foundation** | Booking concepts, PNR structure, understanding system statuses, general verification principles, task logic | Core teaching after validation, with applicability scope stated |
| **Airline / product / fare** | Seat availability, fares, fare rules, taxes and fees, penalties, baggage, services, change/refund conditions, carrier-specific restrictions | Examples or cases linked to a specific carrier/product/market/date; never generalized |
| **Market and time** | Local taxes, market requirements, currency, time-sensitive rules or conditions, changes over time | Context-specific information with date, market, and source; re-verify when needed |
| **Company or travel agency SOP** | Supervisor approval, employee authority limits, contracted or negotiated prices, commission arrangements, agency markups/service fees, workflow sequence, documentation, escalation, communication, and follow-up | Clearly labeled fictional-company policy in a scenario, or a documented policy for a named organization when an authorized source exists |
| **Office / PCC / environment configuration and permissions** | Office settings, user permissions, access to functions or carriers, printing, queues, contractual arrangements, local configurations | A defined and disclosed training environment; never assume all offices or users have the same capabilities |
| **Synthetic training data** | Fictional passenger names, training bookings and PNRs, synthetic prices or inventory, scenario-specific dates or policies | Always labeled as fictional training data; never represented as live data or a real organization's policy |
| **Unverified information** | Old command, uncertain output, conflicting rule, ambiguous title, or insufficient source | Remains on HOLD; do not present as fact or use to judge learner correctness |

### 2.3 “Common” does not mean immutable or universally available

Even information classified as a common foundation must have a defined verification scope. Applicability may vary by:

- Amadeus product, interface, or release.
- Market, airline, or operation type.
- Office/PCC, configuration, and permissions.
- Verification date and subsequent updates.

Operational information should therefore carry, as applicable, its product/environment, market, carrier, office or permission scope, and source or test date. Do not use the label “common” to hide a known limitation.

### 2.4 What learners should learn about variation

It is not enough to present company differences as facts to memorize. Learners should learn how to:

1. Recognize information that needs checking instead of assuming it.
2. Distinguish a system rule from an airline rule, a fare/product condition, a travel-agency policy, and an office configuration.
3. Identify the appropriate source or party to consult.
4. Act within their authority and request approval or escalate when required.
5. Avoid transferring a policy, price, or restriction from one company or case to another without verification.

This is part of transferability across employers, not merely a footnote.

## 3. Simulation and Scenario Requirements

### 3.1 Simulate variation instead of claiming uniformity

DEIXEN may use fictional training data and contexts to demonstrate differences among airlines, markets, travel agencies, and offices. For example:

- Two fictional companies use Amadeus but have different approval and escalation procedures.
- Two tickets have different fare conditions, penalties, or change/refund eligibility.
- An airline or market offers a service or condition that is absent in another case.
- Two employees have different office permissions and cannot perform the same functions.

These are examples for teaching reasoning and adaptation. A fictional-company policy must not be described as a real rule applying to every company.

### 3.2 Clear disclosure in the interface

Where relevant, the learner should see labels such as:

- **Common operational foundation — verified within the stated scope.**
- **Example tied to a specific airline / product / market.**
- **Fictional company policy for training only.**
- **Scenario-specific office setting or permission.**
- **Synthetic training data — not live data.**
- **Unverified — not used as a correctness rule.**

A generic disclaimer on a help page is insufficient when a specific fare, tax, penalty, availability value, or approval policy could be mistaken for live truth. Put the label close to the information itself.

### 3.3 Operational truth is separate from the training layer

- Do not fabricate Amadeus outputs or system responses and make them look authentic.
- Separate verified Amadeus output, synthetic data, and DEIXEN guidance messages.
- If a simulation uses simplified or synthetic output for teaching, explicitly label it as simulated output, not real Amadeus output.
- An LLM must not decide scenario facts, Amadeus truth, system state, correctness, assessment outcomes, or evidence. It may later support natural expression or customer persona within fixed boundaries, but facts, state, and rules remain governed by deterministic, authored, and verified scenario content and rules.
- Unverified information must not be used to teach a command or determine whether the learner is correct.

### 3.4 Scenario consistency and assessment fairness

Fable should design a method that preserves scenario context and variation clearly, so that:

- Learners know when a case uses a fictional company, market, or office.
- Case facts, policies, and synthetic data are defined before the attempt and do not change randomly during assessment.
- Learners are evaluated against the information and authority actually provided to them, not against an undisclosed policy.
- Learners are not penalized for not knowing a company policy that the scenario did not disclose or source.
- Case variants may test transfer, provided the differences and invariants are documented and comparison fairness is checked.
- Adaptation to legitimate variation is not confused with guessing missing information.

The technical implementation is delegated to Fable. Any change to the learning construct, assessment scope, or what DEIXEN treats as competence requires the Founder's approval.

## 4. Validation of the Book and Current Amadeus Behavior

### 4.1 Two separate workstreams

**Track A — Curriculum mapping from the book:** extract potential topics, processes, and skills; understand the book's sequence; identify gaps, repetition, and ambiguous headings.

**Track B — Current operational validation:** verify command syntax, preconditions, output, state changes, errors, and exceptions using current Amadeus references, followed by authorized practical testing when an appropriate environment is available.

Completing Track A does not complete Track B. Likewise, proving that a topic is professionally important does not validate every historical command associated with it.

### 4.2 Required verification record for each command or operation

When validating, record as applicable:

- Topic and command as listed in the book, including page reference.
- Exact command syntax and current source.
- Preconditions and required system state.
- Amadeus product, interface, release, or environment tested.
- Relevant market, carrier, office/PCC, and permissions.
- Date and identity/role of tester or reviewer.
- Tested input and actual observed response, without rewriting it as original verbatim output.
- PNR or ticket state before and after, and whether an actual state change occurred.
- Known errors, warnings, exceptions, and recovery paths.
- Exactly what was established and what remains unproven.
- Status: verified within defined scope / verified with conditions / context-dependent / unverified / conflicting / outdated.
- Source reference and version or access date, with limits on generalization.

Do not generalize a single test result to all markets, offices, or carriers.

### 4.3 When a real Amadeus screen becomes available

The Founder may be able to access a real Amadeus screen soon, but no date is set. Therefore:

- Do not delay curriculum mapping or other work while waiting for this access.
- Prepare a prioritized test list based on Core gaps and the most important topics.
- When access becomes available, first identify whether the environment is training or production and what permissions are granted.
- Test only in an authorized environment and with training or non-sensitive data.
- Do not issue tickets, process refunds or reissues, or perform any action with real financial or operational consequences without explicit permission and an approved safe environment.
- Do not use real passenger or customer booking data in DEIXEN evidence or training samples.
- Record context-specific result differences rather than automatically treating them as errors.

The current absence of a live screen means some results will remain unverified; it does not justify inventing them or lowering the evidence standard.

### 4.4 EV-01 and EV-02 remain separate gates

- **EV-01 — Amadeus operational validation:** command correctness, context, outputs, operational state, errors, and recovery.
- **EV-02 — Practitioner review:** whether the workflow, decisions, customer handling, and outcome represent appropriate professional practice.

A practitioner may contribute to professional review, but that does not replace an authoritative operational source or system test. Likewise, a command's acceptance by the system does not by itself prove professional correctness. Neither the book nor its topic map automatically closes any of EV-01 through EV-06; each gate remains open until its own evidence requirements are met.

Fable should lead the technical and analytical work and present the Founder with a recommendation package containing:

1. **Book Coverage Map:** a complete map of indexed topics with page references, classifications, and links to professional tasks.
2. **Curriculum & Competency Map:** links from topics to knowledge, skills, decisions, and observable performance, then to P1/P2 competencies, with advanced P3 or proposed out-of-scope topics separated.
3. **Transferability Map:** identification of what can be taught as verified common foundation and what depends on airline, market, agency, office/PCC, or permissions.
4. **Verification Ledger:** what has and has not been verified, with source, scope, date, and limits.
5. **Simulation Data & Policy Register:** synthetic data, fictional-company policies, and scenario environment settings, clearly labeled to prevent confusion with reality.
6. **Gap & Conflict Report:** topics missing from the book, ambiguous headings, conflicts, possible updates, and items needing current sources or practitioner input.
7. **Core / Later / Out-of-Scope Recommendation:** proposed priorities, depth, and sequencing, without automatically converting Fable's recommendation into an approved product decision.
8. **Core Completion Implications:** what must be validated, built, and tested before Core can be called complete, distinguishing functional completion from operational and professional validation.

### How Fable should handle recommendations

Fable may decide how to build the map, ledger, simulation engine, validation rules, and file organization. For whether a particular topic enters Core, which professional job the product promises to prepare learners for, or what depth of coverage is acceptable, Fable must recommend and explain, then leave approval to the Founder.

## 6. Prior Decisions Unchanged by This Addendum

This direction does not cancel or weaken previously approved principles, especially:

- The goal is practical professional competence, not command memorization or lesson completion alone.
- Distinguish Amadeus command acceptance, operational correctness, fulfillment of the customer's request, and professional/business correctness.
- Independent competence cannot be inferred from lesson completion, assisted performance, or a correct final state alone.
- Independence, error handling and recovery, and transfer to changed cases must be evidenced under fair assessment controls.
- The three modes remain Practice, Scenario, and Independent Assessment; do not collapse them merely to simplify implementation.
- Coach must not rescue learners in Independent Assessment, and neither Coach nor an LLM may determine Amadeus truth or assessment outcomes.
- Separate authentic operational output from DEIXEN's training messages.
- Simulated-performance evidence is evidence of performance in a simulation, not actual work experience, an accredited professional certificate, or a hiring guarantee.
- Previously approved privacy, learner-controlled sharing, and traceable result-correction principles remain in force.
- Unverified matters remain explicitly labeled; gaps must not be filled by guessing.
