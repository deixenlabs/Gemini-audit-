## How to use this document

This document records the objectives that have emerged from the DEIXEN discussions. It separates three things that must not be confused:

1. **DEIXEN's objectives:** what the product and, eventually, the training business/institution are meant to achieve.
2. **The founder's business and institutional ambitions:** what the owner hopes to build around DEIXEN over the long term.
3. **The founder's personal career objective:** the owner's own near-term professional goal, which must remain separate from DEIXEN's target markets and product strategy.

It also distinguishes established direction from future ambition and unvalidated hypotheses. A goal recorded here is not automatically an approved feature, technical architecture, commercial commitment, or claim that the current product already achieves it.

## 1. DEIXEN's overarching mission

**Develop real professional capability and reduce the gap between what a person learns in a course and what they are expected to do when they enter an actual travel company.**

DEIXEN should help a learner progress from beginner-level knowledge toward performing defined travel-operations work tasks with increasing competence and as much independence as can be responsibly developed and demonstrated.

The objective is not merely to teach commands, provide course completion, or simulate a booking. It is to help people understand, decide, execute, verify, communicate, recover from errors, and complete professional work.

DEIXEN must not claim that course completion or simulation guarantees employment, full workplace readiness, or expert status. It should make evidence-based, task-specific statements about what a learner has actually demonstrated and under what conditions.

## 2. DEIXEN's product and learner objectives

### 2.1 Build demonstrable professional competence

The product should help learners develop the ability to:

- Understand a customer's request, context, needs, constraints, and missing information.
- Ask relevant questions and clarify ambiguous or incomplete requests.
- Communicate clearly and professionally with different customer types.
- Explain appropriate options and make or support sound decisions.
- Carry out relevant travel-reservation work using the supported GDS environment.
- Verify that the operational result actually satisfies the customer's request.
- Identify mistakes, warnings, inconsistencies, and risks.
- Correct errors or know when escalation is appropriate.
- Communicate outcomes, manage expectations, close tasks, and follow up where needed.
- Transfer their knowledge to changed or unfamiliar task variants rather than only repeat memorized steps.

### 2.2 Close the training-to-work gap

DEIXEN should connect learning to recognizable professional work rather than teach disconnected facts and commands.

The proposed primary unit of learning and assessment is the **Professional Work Task**, which can include:

Customer request → clarification and discovery → advice / decision / confirmation → GDS execution → task validation → recovery / escalation → customer communication → closure / follow-up.

This is a strategic design direction to validate and formalize in the Master Blueprint, not a claim that every part is already implemented.

### 2.3 Develop independence progressively

A central objective is to move learners from supported performance toward independent performance.

The intended progression is:

Learning → Demonstration / Ghost → Guided Practice → Independent Practice → Scenarios → Independent Assessment → Evidence → Growth / Continuing Development.

The system should:

- Make the level and type of assistance visible in the evidence.
- Reduce guidance progressively as the learner develops.
- Distinguish performance with hints, Ghost, or Coach assistance from independent performance.
- Test whether the learner can recover from errors and handle changed circumstances.
- Assess transfer to unpractised task variants.
- Avoid making the learner permanently dependent on the Coach.

**Independence is a core outcome, not an optional advanced feature.**

### 2.4 Teach operational correctness and professional correctness separately

DEIXEN should never equate a syntactically correct command or system-accepted transaction with a professionally correct result.

At minimum, assessments and learning design should distinguish:

1. **Input / syntax correctness:** whether the entered command or data is valid.
2. **Operational correctness:** whether the system accepted the action and reached the intended operational state.
3. **Customer / task correctness:** whether the result satisfies the actual customer request and case constraints.
4. **Professional / business correctness:** whether the decision and handling were appropriate, responsible, and well communicated.

Customer satisfaction is relevant but must not substitute for operational or professional correctness. A customer can be satisfied while the work is wrong, and a system can accept a booking that does not satisfy the request.

### 2.5 Use a credible and progressively challenging learning method

The learning experience should be based on sound learning and assessment principles, with methods validated rather than adopted as decoration.

Objectives include:

- Explain concepts before demanding unsupported execution.
- Use worked examples and meaningful demonstrations.
- Encourage prediction, recall, and understanding of why a step is required.
- Provide guided practice and then fade assistance.
- Use realistic, varied, and progressively challenging tasks.
- Revisit important knowledge over time and assess delayed retention where appropriate.
- Test transfer to new scenarios, not only repetition of taught examples.
- Distinguish usability, learner satisfaction, learning effectiveness, professional validity, and commercial viability.

### 2.6 Support both current core priorities

- **Priority 1:** entry-level competence needed to begin performing relevant travel-operations work.
- **Priority 2:** additional capabilities that help beginners work more confidently, reliably, and professionally.
- **Priority 3:** advanced specializations, explicitly future scope until P1 and P2 have been implemented and validated successfully.

P1 and P2 are both core priorities now. P3 must not expand or delay the initial scope.

### 2.7 Make English a practical workplace capability

A major product objective is to help learners improve the English used in travel operations and customer service through meaningful work situations, not only through separate grammar or vocabulary lessons.

The current direction for DEIXEN World is **English-first**:

- Coach and customer interactions should normally use clear, natural professional English.
- Work instructions, common phrases, and travel terminology should appear in context.
- Learners should hear, read, and practise language while handling professional tasks.
- Captions, replay, and appropriate language support should aid comprehension.
- Arabic explanations may be available as optional support without turning the main experience into an Arabic-only environment.
- Learners should not be penalized merely for having an Egyptian, Arabic, or other understandable accent. Assessment should focus on intelligibility and successful professional communication, with criteria defined and validated.
- Text must remain a viable alternative to voice input; speech recognition and accent differences must not make access or assessment unfair.

This is a product objective. It is not a statement that the founder personally has an English-learning goal.

### 2.8 Create an engaging professional learning experience

DEIXEN should make demanding professional learning motivating and memorable without sacrificing substance.

The proposed DEIXEN World direction is a story-driven, cinematic, interactive workplace environment in which learners experience professional situations, understand the context, and then take responsibility for the work.

Potential experience goals include:

- A believable virtual travel-company environment.
- Story-led case introductions and customer situations.
- Cinematic scenes that establish context, show customer behaviour, or introduce urgent events.
- Camera transitions between the customer, employee, and workstation.
- Recurring characters and connected story arcs where they add learning value.
- Dynamic events tied to the state of the task.
- A world that feels engaging to watch as well as meaningful to participate in.

Entertainment must serve professional understanding, motivation, and practice. Cut scenes must not reveal hidden case facts, give away assessment answers, or replace learner performance. Scenes should be purposeful, reasonably concise, and offer appropriate replay / skip and reduced-motion support.

### 2.9 Establish the Coach as a professional development supervisor

The Coach should be more than a tooltip system or a helper that waits for a question.

The agreed priority direction is **Coach as a Virtual Professional Supervisor** who:

- Welcomes and orients the learner in the virtual company.
- Explains workplace responsibilities and expectations.
- Introduces and frames tasks without revealing their solutions.
- Teaches concepts and demonstrates work when appropriate.
- Observes learning performance and identifies recurring difficulties.
- Uses questions and graduated guidance instead of doing the work for the learner.
- Reviews decisions and performance after practice tasks.
- Helps the learner identify what to improve next.
- Reduces assistance progressively as the learner becomes more capable.

Cinematic scenes are a high-priority way to express the Coach's supervisory role and make the relationship feel real. This does not mean all scenes must feature the Coach; customer stories, emergencies, workplace events, and outcomes can also be shown cinematically.

The Coach must not provide prohibited hints or rescue the learner during protected independent assessment.

The following must be designed as distinct concerns:

- Coach character / avatar and voice.
- Coach behaviour, instructional logic, and assistance policy.
- Cinematic scene system used to present the Coach and other characters.

The visual avatar and voice are not yet final. A non-human / robot-like reading is acceptable for exploration if it strengthens the identity of a professional simulator and virtual workplace.

### 2.10 Build a trustworthy customer and workplace simulation

The customer should behave as a coherent simulated person, not an unrestricted chatbot that can invent case facts.

The desired simulation should include:

- Defined customer intent, needs, preferences, constraints, and hidden facts.
- Consistent answers to relevant questions.
- A personality and communication style that influence dialogue without changing case truth.
- Emotional states and reactions that respond appropriately to the interaction.
- A clear separation between what the learner can observe and what must be discovered.
- Support for text first and voice where technically and pedagogically justified.

AI may help with natural language, voice, and personality expression, but authoritative case facts, operational GDS behaviour, and assessment rules must remain controlled by deterministic or otherwise validated sources of truth.

The Customer and Coach must remain distinct roles: the customer provides the request and reacts; the Coach teaches and supervises.

### 2.11 Provide authentic operational practice without false claims

The initial product focus is Amadeus. The operational environment must reproduce only behaviour that has been implemented and verified.

- Clearly distinguish a training simulator from a live Amadeus system.
- Do not invent commands, responses, restrictions, or operational outcomes.
- Keep system output and training explanations visibly distinct.
- Validate task correctness independently of system acceptance.
- Expand GDS coverage only after the initial priorities have been implemented and validated; do not introduce multi-GDS scope prematurely.

### 2.12 Produce credible evidence of capability

DEIXEN should show what a learner has demonstrated, not manufacture a vague readiness score.

Evidence should be traceable and may include:

- The task and case variant attempted.
- The operational actions and sequence.
- The resulting system state.
- Errors, warnings, and recovery actions.
- Customer interaction and decisions.
- Whether assistance, Ghost, or Coach intervention occurred.
- Whether the required task criteria were met.
- What was and was not assessed.
- Whether the performance was independent and under what conditions.

Avoid numerical readiness percentages or a single score that hides critical failures. Use descriptive evidence and explicit limitations. Do not imply that simulated evidence alone proves workplace performance in every real-world setting.

A future Shift Evidence Record may document performance across multiple customers and connected tasks in a simulated work shift.

### 2.13 Support growth after initial training and employment

DEIXEN should not become useless once a learner obtains a job.

The longer-term objective is to support continuing professional development through valuable new cases, refreshers, procedural updates, more complex situations, and role progression. A recurring membership or renewal model is only justified if this continuing value is real and regularly maintained.

### 2.14 Serve a broader market without confusing it with the founder's personal plans

DEIXEN's potential market is international. Saudi Arabia, Oman, and the UAE are important markets, and Egypt is also within the potential market and may be useful for early pilots.

The product is not exclusively for Saudi Arabia. The founder's personal interest in working in Saudi Arabia must not narrow DEIXEN's long-term market definition.

The current customer-development sequence discussed is:

1. Trainees / learners.
2. Employees seeking improvement or continuing development.
3. Companies and organisations.

This is a strategic direction to evaluate, not a fully validated go-to-market plan.

### 2.15 Build a sustainable business around real value

Commercial objectives include:

- Establish a differentiated offer based on integrated professional work competence, not a feature list or AI roleplay alone.
- Provide a meaningful entry-level free Explore experience where appropriate.
- Offer paid access to a coherent and valuable training foundation.
- Give former learners a real reason to continue using DEIXEN after employment.
- Test willingness to pay before treating proposed prices or subscriptions as settled.
- Explore learner sales, educational partnerships, training-provider relationships, and later organisational sales.
- Validate commercial sustainability separately from learning effectiveness and product quality.

Previously discussed price points are hypotheses only and must not be treated as approved prices.

### 2.16 Make the complete user experience and workflow understandable

DEIXEN must not only contain useful pages and features; it must guide the learner through them as one coherent, low-friction professional learning experience.

The objective is to place the complexity of the product's operational, educational, narrative, and assessment systems behind a clear learner experience:

**Complexity belongs in the system, not in the learner's experience.**

The product should:

- Make it clear where the learner is, what mode they are in, and what the next useful action is.
- Connect Home, Learning, Terminal, Scenario Bank, and Growth without forcing the learner to understand the system's internal architecture.
- Preserve task context when moving between customer interaction, cinematic scenes, and Terminal.
- Use progressive disclosure so the learner sees the relevant instructions and controls at the right time.
- Provide understandable routes for errors, corrections, interruptions, pausing, resuming, and unsuccessful attempts.
- Make Coach and Ghost assistance consistent with the learner's current mode and independence objective.
- Support accessibility, captions, replay, reduced motion, and text alternatives to voice.
- Avoid unnecessary steps, duplicated information, confusing navigation, and decorative complexity.

The current Learning → Demonstration / Ghost → Guided Practice → Independent Practice → Scenarios → Independent Assessment → Evidence → Growth sequence is a pedagogical framework, not a complete UX specification. The end-to-end user journeys, page flows, task flows, state transitions, and recovery paths must be audited and designed before implementation.

A workflow is successful only when learners can navigate and perform the work with understandable guidance and manageable cognitive load. Enjoyment, visual quality, fewer clicks, or completion speed alone do not establish UX quality or learning effectiveness.

**Status:** Core product objective. The detailed workflow remains open for audit, design, and usability validation.

## 3. The founder's business and institutional ambitions

These are broader ambitions around building DEIXEN. They are not all current product requirements.

### 3.1 Build and launch a serious, competitive product

The founder wants DEIXEN to compete credibly with existing training products and providers. The objective is not to release quickly at the expense of quality. The product should have a distinctive professional purpose, trustworthy content, coherent experience, and evidence of value.

### 3.2 Build a long-term specialised vocational training institution

The long-term ambition is larger than operating a software subscription business: establish a specialised vocational training institution for travel / aviation operations that uses DEIXEN's proprietary software as a core training environment.

This may involve training learners directly, supporting employees, and eventually serving organisations. Licensing, accreditation, legal requirements, and operational feasibility in each country must be researched before making commitments.

### 3.3 Explore a future relationship with Amadeus

The founder has an ambition to pursue possible cooperation or partnership with Amadeus in the future. This is an aspiration, not an existing relationship, entitlement, endorsement, or guaranteed outcome.

The product should first establish professional validity, technical credibility, and a legitimate training proposition. Any outreach, partnership, certification, or use of proprietary materials must respect permission, licensing, and affiliation boundaries.

### 3.4 Build an asset that can grow beyond the initial narrow product slice

The founder wants a product foundation that can expand responsibly: more validated professional cases, greater task complexity, additional customer and workplace situations, continuing development, and potentially additional GDS capabilities after the initial priorities succeed.

The ambition is extensibility without premature scope expansion.

## 4. The founder's personal career objective

### 4.1 Near-term professional objective

The founder has a personal near-term goal of seeking work in Saudi Arabia. This explains the importance of understanding Saudi employer expectations and entry-level travel-operations job requirements in the current research.

This objective should be treated separately from DEIXEN's product strategy:

- Saudi Arabia is personally important to the founder's near-term career plans.
- DEIXEN is intended to serve a broader international market.
- The founder's personal job-search needs must not automatically determine the product's entire curriculum, brand, customer base, or geographic scope.
- Research into Saudi vacancies and employer expectations can inform DEIXEN where it is transferable and supported by evidence, but must be distinguished from broader market validation.

### 4.2 Become DEIXEN's first real learner — Founder-Learner Pilot

The founder wants to use DEIXEN personally, not only supervise its development. This comes from direct experience of the training-to-work gap: the founder wants to understand the GDS system more deeply and improve practical ability through the product being built.

This creates a personal learning objective and a valuable early validation route:

- Be the first real learner to use DEIXEN through a defined learning journey.
- Establish an honest baseline of current GDS understanding and practical ability before training.
- Use the Learning tracks, Ghost, Coach, Terminal, and scenarios as they become sufficiently usable.
- Record where instructions are unclear, where navigation is confusing, where the Coach helps or fails to help, and where the product does not support the learner's actual needs.
- Attempt independent tasks and changed/unfamiliar cases to test whether knowledge transfers beyond rehearsed steps.
- Record what the founder can and cannot demonstrate after training, including the conditions and assistance involved.
- Use the experience to improve the product, but do not assume that one founder's learning preferences represent all beginners.
- Seek independent practitioner review of professional actions and outcomes; the founder's own confidence or the product's internal assessment is not sufficient evidence of competence.

This pilot has two distinct values:

1. **Personal value:** help the founder develop real understanding and practical capability in the GDS and travel-operations work.
2. **Product value:** expose friction, missing instruction, weak workflows, unrealistic scenarios, and gaps in evidence before testing with other learners.

The Founder-Learner Pilot is an owner-supported validation objective. It is not a claim that DEIXEN already teaches effectively, and it must not replace testing with independent beginners and professional practitioners.

### 4.3 Personal goals not yet established in the available context

The available context does not establish a separate goal for the founder to become an English teacher, pursue a specific language certificate, or personally master every advanced GDS specialisation. These should not be added as personal commitments unless the founder states them. The founder's desire for DEIXEN to teach practical professional English is a product objective, distinct from a personal career or certification goal.

## 5. The founder's preferred way of building DEIXEN

These are working and governance preferences that affect how objectives should be pursued.

- Prefer critical, evidence-based disagreement over reassurance or automatic agreement.
- Explain technical matters in accessible Egyptian Arabic when discussing them with the founder.
- Separate verified facts, inferences, hypotheses, proposals, and owner-approved decisions.
- Do not claim that a feature exists merely because it appears in a document.
- Do not claim that a repository, file, test suite, or source has been inspected when it has not.
- Preserve read-only boundaries during audits. No repository or knowledge-file changes without explicit owner approval.
- Do not turn an AI model's recommendation into an approved decision.
- Keep independent audits independent until all reviewers have completed and frozen their work.
- Build the final strategy and canonical documentation structure before implementation.
- Optimise for the smallest complete, coherent, unambiguous, traceable documentation system—not the largest number of files.
- Prepare a clear, evidence-backed Master Blueprint and Opus-ready execution package before asking Opus to implement the product.
- Do not let speed or visual spectacle outrank professional validity, learning effectiveness, operational correctness, accessibility, maintainability, or long-term strategic coherence.
- Treat end-to-end UX and workflow architecture as a first-class product discipline, not a cosmetic pass after features are designed. Review page journeys, task flows, modes, state transitions, error recovery, cognitive load, and accessibility before implementation.
- Use the founder as an early real learner through a documented Founder-Learner Pilot, while avoiding overfitting the product to one person's needs and validating later with independent beginners and practitioners.
- Prefer a coherent and simple learner experience even when the underlying system requires complex orchestration.
- The current logo and visual identity are not approved. The prior `04 Design System` and `DEIXEN — Identity Closure` materials have been rejected as visual-identity references and are to be removed from the active repository by the owner. Future identity work should begin as a new exploration. This does not automatically invalidate unrelated UX or functional information in other materials; those must be assessed on their own merits.

## 6. Non-goals and boundaries

The following are not the intended objectives:

- Becoming a generic LMS or a collection of video courses.
- Teaching only command syntax without customer and task context.
- Treating a completed booking as sufficient proof of competence.
- Treating customer satisfaction as proof of professional correctness.
- Treating AI-generated dialogue or a 3D world as differentiation by itself.
- Making voice interaction mandatory when it creates accessibility or accent unfairness.
- Allowing an LLM to invent authoritative customer facts or GDS behaviour.
- Making the learner dependent on the Coach.
- Exposing internal system complexity to the learner through fragmented pages, confusing mode changes, unnecessary navigation, or disconnected feature workflows.
- Treating the current high-level learning sequence as a finished UX workflow without journey mapping and usability validation.
- Using a numerical readiness score as a substitute for evidence.
- Building advanced specialisations or multiple GDS platforms before P1 and P2 succeed.
- Building a large virtual world before proving the value of the core professional task experience.
- Claiming employment guarantees, official Amadeus affiliation, or workplace competence beyond the evidence.
- Treating the founder's Saudi job objective as a restriction on DEIXEN's geographic ambitions.

## 7. Goal status and decision discipline

Use the following status distinctions when this document is used in future planning:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Core objective** | A fundamental product or founder objective already stated clearly and repeatedly. |
| **Agreed direction** | The owner has expressed agreement with the direction, but detailed design, validation, or implementation remains open. |
| **Long-term ambition** | A desired future outcome that is not a current delivery commitment. |
| **Hypothesis / proposal** | A concept that must be tested and may be changed or rejected. |
| **Not established** | The available context does not support treating it as one of the owner's goals. |

Examples:

- Reducing the training-to-work gap: **Core objective**.
- Developing independence through progressive reduction of help: **Core objective**.
- Coach as a cinematic professional supervisor: **Agreed direction / high-priority design direction**, with implementation details still open.
- English-first DEIXEN World: **Agreed direction to develop and validate**.
- Story-driven cinematic Scenario Bank / DEIXEN World: **Strategic design hypothesis with strong owner interest**, requiring validation of learning value and production feasibility.
- End-to-end UX/workflow as a first-class design workstream: **Core product objective**, with complete flows still to be audited and designed.
- Founder-Learner Pilot: **Owner-supported personal and product-validation objective**, not a substitute for independent learner/practitioner validation.
- A specialised vocational institution: **Long-term founder ambition**.
- Amadeus partnership: **Long-term founder aspiration, not an existing relationship**.
- Seeking work in Saudi Arabia: **Founder personal near-term career objective**.
- Exact subscription prices: **Unvalidated commercial hypotheses**.

## 8. How this reference should be used with the Product & Feature Ideas Inventory

These two documents serve different purposes:

- **Objectives Reference:** explains why DEIXEN exists, what outcomes it should produce, and what the founder is trying to achieve.
- **Product & Feature Ideas Inventory:** records the pages, features, workflows, and experience ideas that may help deliver those outcomes.

A feature should not be retained merely because it is attractive or technically possible. It should be linked to one or more objectives in this document, have a clear user and professional benefit, and be evaluated for cost, dependencies, evidence, and priority.

During the three independent audits and subsequent synthesis, recommendations should be evaluated against these objectives without treating every proposed feature as mandatory. The final Master Blueprint should resolve conflicts, define measurable validation criteria, and separate current commitments from future ambitions.
