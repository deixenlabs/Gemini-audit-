## 0. Authority, Scope, and Use

This document is a proposal for the founder to review. It does not replace, rewrite, or amend the current Working Source of Truth, the v2.6 review package, the original UX/workflow register, the Argon deliverables, the Sonnet reports, the application repository, or the founder's owner-direction record.

No application code, Git repository, branch, source document, or Library file was edited as part of preparing this proposal. The proposal is intended to make the next decision explicit: **which findings should be retained, corrected, adapted, rejected, deferred, or delegated to Fable 5.2**.

### 0.1 Authority labels used here

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Owner-approved direction** | A direction explicitly approved by the founder. Preserve it unless the founder later approves a change. |
| **Owner-endorsed strategic candidate** | The founder has confirmed the strategic importance of an idea and asked that it be evaluated/developed. This is not blanket approval of its complete specification, implementation, timing, or cost. |
| **Retain** | Keep the existing principle or rule substantially as written. Clarification may still be needed. |
| **Correct / rewrite** | The underlying intent is sound, but the wording, logic, ownership, or testability needs correction. |
| **Adapt** | Preserve the useful part of a recommendation, but do not adopt it exactly as proposed. |
| **Reject as proposed** | Do not adopt the proposal in its present form. The underlying problem may still deserve treatment. |
| **Defer** | Keep visible as future work or a later validation item; do not make it a present build dependency. |
| **Delegate to Fable** | Fable should investigate and recommend the design/technical solution. This does not authorize a material scope change without founder approval. |
| **Needs external evidence** | Documentary comparison cannot settle the matter. A source, practitioner, learner, employer, legal, or technical test is needed. |

### 0.2 Core consolidation rule

**Simplify complexity in the specification and implementation only when the meaning, evidence, fairness, and owner-approved safeguards remain intact.** A shorter document is not automatically a better design if it hides a state transition, weakens an assessment boundary, or makes the result impossible to explain.

## 2. Executive Consolidation Recommendation

The three reviews are broadly aligned on DEIXEN's professional mission and the importance of connecting customer interaction, operational execution, professional correctness, independence, and evidence. The disagreements are mainly about **how much assessment machinery to preserve now, how to represent execution lifecycles, and how quickly to reduce the specification before the first real beginner and practitioner tests**.

The recommended consolidation is:

1. **Preserve the product's full professional ambition.** Do not reduce DEIXEN to a single Amadeus command exercise, an LMS, an AI customer chatbot, or a small reservation-only product identity.
2. **Preserve the founder-approved development sequence.** Complete a real, usable local Core within a defined first-release scope; test it; run the founder/beginner pilot; obtain independent professional validation; build the Backend and production protections before public launch; scope World/LLM separately.
3. **Keep the three Terminal modes distinct.** Practice, Scenario, and Independent Assessment remain three modes. Assistance policies are defined within each mode; they must not replace the modes.
4. **Retain the professional-task logic in Workflow v2.4 Stages 90–106**, while adapting CP-001 to include the founder-approved bounded advice-and-choice step. CP-001 remains on HOLD pending modification and validation.
5. **Do not adopt Sonnet's structural simplifications wholesale.** Some are good candidates for better wording or simpler implementation, but merging Task Run into Attempt, reducing exposure to one generic reveal record, or collapsing status dimensions could damage auditability and assessment fairness.
6. **Correct the result-defect path.** Keep a deterministic recheck under the original rule version, but distinguish that from a product-team investigation of a suspected rule defect. The founder has authorized the product/development team to correct confirmed defects through new rule versions and additive, traceable impact-scope re-evaluation.
7. **Create a competency and evidence foundation before polishing Growth.** The P1/P2 competency map, case-to-skill links, evidence semantics, and professional closure criteria are foundational gaps. Avoid building a visually persuasive evidence profile on top of undefined skills or unclear evidence rules.
8. **Carry the three professional-evidence additions forward as owner-endorsed strategic candidates:** Evidence Summary / potential Professional Evidence Profile, internal Case & Provenance Validator, and internal Practitioner Review Export. Their importance is endorsed; detailed specs, delivery order, costs, and release scope remain open.
9. **Treat the competitive-positioning report as a useful hypothesis generator, not a market verdict.** Its integrated-experience thesis is promising, but differentiation, employer acceptance, buyer route, regional demand, and willingness to pay remain unproven.
10. **Give Fable the design and technical choices that belong with the project lead.** Do not ask the founder to settle internal implementation details unless a choice materially changes product scope, commitments, cost, or user experience.

### 2.1 Recommended disposition vocabulary

For consolidation purposes, every recommendation and open point should be assigned one of these dispositions: **Retain / Correct / Adapt / Reject as proposed / Defer / Delegate to Fable / Needs external evidence**. Do not record recommendations as owner decisions simply because they appear in an audit report or this proposal.

## 3. What Should Be Retained as the Shared Baseline

The following are the strongest common principles across the source documents and should remain central in the consolidated baseline.

### 3.1 Product identity and professional task

DEIXEN is a professional travel-operations learning and simulated-workplace product. Its primary learning and assessment unit should be a meaningful professional task, not a disconnected command, lesson, quiz, or generic simulation interaction.

The full task concept should cover, where relevant to the task:

Customer request → discovery and clarification → advice / choice → confirmation and authorization → operational execution → validation against the customer's request → correction / recovery / pause / escalation → customer communication → closure / follow-up.

A task may branch, pause, be declined, or end without a booking. A professionally correct stop is not automatically a failed outcome.

### 3.2 Four kinds of correctness

Keep distinct:

1. **Input / syntax correctness** — whether the learner entered a valid command or data.
2. **Operational correctness** — whether the system accepted the operation and reached the expected operational state.
3. **Customer / task correctness** — whether the result actually satisfies the customer's request and constraints.
4. **Professional / business correctness** — whether the learner's advice, decision, communication, handling, and escalation were appropriate.

Customer satisfaction does not replace task correctness. System acceptance does not prove the customer received the right outcome.

### 3.3 Learning and independence progression

Retain the connected development journey:

Learning → Demonstration / Ghost → Guided Practice → Independent Practice → Scenarios → Independent Assessment → Evidence → Growth / continuing development.

However, distinguish the learning progression from the Terminal's three execution modes. Independent Practice is not automatically independent assessment evidence. A correct final state does not prove that the learner performed the work independently.

### 3.4 Five-area product structure and cross-product capabilities

Preserve the five-area structure as the current baseline:

- Home (final naming remains open where not owner-confirmed).
- Learning.
- Terminal.
- Scenario Bank.
- Growth.

Coach and Ghost remain cross-product capabilities. DEIXEN World / Virtual Company remains future scope within Scenario Bank, not a sixth top-level area. Scenario Bank organizes the case; Terminal is where the task runs.

### 3.5 Operational truth and deterministic control

Retain the rules that:

- Amadeus commands, outputs, operational state, customer facts, task correctness, assessment rules, and evidence decisions must not be invented by an LLM.
- Unknown or unverified Amadeus behaviour must remain visibly unverified or on HOLD.
- Authentic Amadeus output must remain separate from DEIXEN instruction or feedback.
- The customer simulation must remain bounded by authored scenario facts and deterministic rules, even if a later LLM helps express natural language or personality.

### 3.6 Evidence integrity and non-destructive correction

Retain:

- Help requested and help delivered as separate facts.
- Event history and result records with traceable versions.
- Original results and prior rule versions preserved when corrections occur.
- A correction updates the current accepted conclusion without rewriting what happened historically.
- Privacy by default and voluntary learner-controlled sharing.
- No readiness percentages, employment guarantees, certification claims, or unapproved Amadeus-affiliation claims.

### 3.7 NOW / LATER / FUTURE / OUT OF SCOPE

Keep the sequence and boundaries:

- **NOW:** local Frontend and deterministic engine, usable complete Core within a defined scope, local validation and pilot.
- **LATER:** Backend, durable persistence, accounts/permissions, server-side validation and tamper protection, production security and reliability before public launch.
- **FUTURE:** World, 3D/cinematics, voice, LLM-supported expression, multi-customer shifts, and additional GDSs if justified.
- **OUT OF CURRENT SOFTWARE SCOPE:** human trainer/HR/institutional governance and administration workflows unless separately reopened by the founder.

Deferring Backend does not cancel it. Deferring World/LLM does not mean the capabilities are rejected forever.

## 4. Sonnet First Audit — Recommendation-by-Recommendation Disposition

This section addresses the first Sonnet report's R-series recommendations and related high-priority gaps. The dispositions below are proposed for founder review.

### 4.1 R-01 — Replace exposure model with one reveal record

**Disposition: ADAPT; do not adopt the single-record simplification as stated.**

Sonnet correctly identified that the v2.4 exposure section could become a costly classification system before the first validated case and learner test exist. Argon correctly distinguished observable technical events from the policy consequences for assessment independence.

Recommended consolidation:

- Keep observable concepts separate: content delivered to the client; content rendered/displayed; content explicitly opened by the learner; and the audit record that records the relevant exposure event.
- Do not claim that telemetry proves comprehension or that a displayed item was mentally understood.
- Keep the policy decision separate from the telemetry: which skills or downstream decisions may have been causally affected by the exposure?
- Preserve causal dependency: a downstream decision is not independent if it depends on an exposed upstream fact, even if the skills are listed under different clusters.
- Do not build browser surveillance, proctoring, an LLM exposure classifier, or a broad adjudication system.
- Fable should define the smallest first-case exposure metadata and tests. Partial-exposure and fine-grained skill-impact machinery may be phased only if the first real cases and learner tests show that a coarser model cannot protect evidence quality.

**Reason:** simplify the authoring and implementation burden without confusing delivered, displayed, opened, recorded, and policy-affected states.

### 4.2 R-02 — Result recheck and rule-defect correction

**Disposition: RETAIN the diagnostic distinction; CORRECT the workflow to reflect the owner-approved team correction path.**

A recheck under the original case, event, and rule versions can test reproducibility or detect a calculation/data mismatch. It cannot discover that the original assessment rule itself was wrong.

Recommended consolidation:

1. Keep an automated recheck against the original data and original rule version.
2. Report what that check can establish and what it cannot establish.
3. Separate at least three situations: no reproducibility mismatch; a reproducible calculation/data defect; and insufficient data to verify automatically.
4. If a rule defect is suspected, the product/development team investigates it outside the automated recheck.
5. If confirmed, release a corrected rule version, define the impact scope, and re-evaluate every potentially affected attempt/outcome in scope, including attempts without a learner report where the defect applies.
6. Preserve the original result, original rule version, correction reason, corrected version, and links to all affected attempts. Do not create a second performance attempt or erase the original history.

The founder has approved a limited internal product-quality correction role. This does not create a trainer, HR, committee, or institutional appeals workflow.

**Reason:** the user-visible status must not claim that a machine discovered or corrected a rule defect when that judgment was made by the product team.

### 4.3 R-03 — Merge Stages 144–149 into three rules

**Disposition: ADAPT. Simplify the explanatory structure only; preserve all six semantic protections and independently testable outcomes.**

It may be reasonable to group these stages under three headings:

- result correction and historical preservation;
- recalculation of dependent derived views and recommendations;
- prospective eligibility changes and protection of started/completed attempts.

But the consolidation must explicitly preserve:

- corrected result becomes the current accepted result for the same Attempt, with prior result retained as linked history;
- dependent Growth/competency/recommendation/access outputs are recalculated appropriately;
- superseded recommendations remain in history and are not silently rewritten;
- eligibility changes apply prospectively to activities not yet started;
- a started attempt retains its starting eligibility and rule snapshot unless its own basis/result is defective;
- a completed valid attempt is not automatically invalidated by a later correction to a prerequisite;
- competency is recalculated from the relevant valid evidence, without counting original and corrected result records as two performances.

**Do not delete Stage 147 or Stage 148 semantics.** They are explicit owner-approved behavior and are among the highest-priority safeguards.

### 4.4 R-04 — Replace overlapping status lists with three fields

**Disposition: ADAPT; reject the three-field model as an immediate replacement until a full status map proves equivalence.**

Sonnet correctly identified overlapping labels and inconsistent terms. But three fields may be too few if they merge different concepts such as attempt lifecycle, task outcome, performance quality, evidence validity, per-skill result, independence/assistance, eligibility, or correction workflow.

Recommended approach:

- Build one canonical status dictionary and ownership map.
- Separate status dimensions that answer different questions.
- Define each status once with: meaning, allowed transitions, who/what sets it, whether it is historical or current, and what UI text is derived from it.
- Keep Task Outcome separate from Performance Outcome.
- Distinguish Not Assessed from Not Demonstrated. Do not use Not Applicable as a substitute for either unless a genuinely different semantic case exists and is explicitly defined.
- Display labels may be simplified for learners, but the underlying state must remain precise.

Fable may reduce the number of stored fields after mapping the complete existing vocabulary and proving that no distinct fact or test is lost.

### 4.5 R-05 — Merge Task Run into Attempt

**Disposition: REJECT AS A PRE-APPROVED CHANGE; DELEGATE THE IMPLEMENTATION CHOICE TO FABLE.**

The reports identify a real naming/overlap problem. However, the source material gives the concepts different responsibilities:

- Task Run: mutable live execution context, including Conversation State, Task State, active mode, assistance policy, and interaction with the operational session.
- Attempt: formal opportunity and performance/evidence record with a frozen starting snapshot, event history, lifecycle, and versioned results.
- Operational Session: isolated operational state for the working PNR and saved records.

The consolidation should preserve these logical responsibilities unless Fable demonstrates a simpler representation that still protects them. One implementation object or aggregate may be acceptable if the sub-responsibilities, ownership, lifecycle, and event semantics remain separately testable. Do not force five code classes solely because Argon proposed five conceptual objects, and do not merge them solely because Sonnet observed overlapping words.

**Required Fable output:** a lifecycle diagram/table showing object creation, mutation, pause/resume, submission, finalization, correction, and ownership of each record.

### 4.6 R-06 — Merge Stages 153–155 into Stage 150

**Disposition: ADAPT. Group the prose if useful, but retain all six start-boundary contracts and their tests.**

The six guarantees must remain explicit:

1. Previewing does not consume an Attempt.
2. No attempt-governed command runs before the Attempt is committed.
3. Repeated start requests reuse one logical request identity and do not create duplicate attempts.
4. Eligibility changes and start requests are ordered at the commit boundary.
5. Open previews refresh and block start when eligibility is lost.
6. Restored eligibility permits a new start but does not auto-start the Attempt.

The Program Engine must recompute eligibility from current valid evidence at the start boundary; it must not trust a UI-supplied verdict. Backend-level atomicity/concurrency guarantees remain LATER, but the semantic contract and local sequential tests remain NOW.

### 4.7 R-07 — Archive Stages 61–89

**Disposition: ADAPT; remove human-administration scope from the active product plan, but do not bulk-delete or blindly archive the history.**

The founder's current direction excludes trainer, HR, institutional administration, dispute committees, and broad human review workflows from current software scope. However, preserve useful principles before reorganizing the register:

- Stage 59: private by default and voluntary learner-controlled sharing.
- Stage 60: additive, non-destructive records and preserved history.
- Relevant Stage 64–68 principles: automated recommendations remain advisory and never substitute for performance evidence; learner response is not proof; attributable actions and transparent rationale where relevant.
- Least-privilege and context-limited access as general privacy principles, not as a commitment to build a trainer portal.

Recommended disposition for the register: split mixed rows into **retain as product principle**, **OUT OF CURRENT SCOPE**, and **archive/history reference**. Preserve original stage references. Do not move or remove a block until every potentially reusable principle has been extracted and mapped.

### 4.8 R-08 — Six bounded modules and one writer per record

**Disposition: ADAPT. Retain bounded responsibilities and single-writer ownership as design principles; do not lock six modules before code inspection.**

The Program Engine should not become an undefined mega-engine. Every authoritative record should have a clearly identified writer/owner. Content, operational state, conversation/task state, assessment/evidence, and persistence responsibilities should be sufficiently separated.

Fable should determine whether six modules, fewer modules, or a different arrangement best fits the actual application. The local persistence boundary should be replaceable so the Backend can be added later without rebuilding semantics.

### 4.9 R-09 — Modes as assistance presets; assessment entry from Scenario Bank

**Disposition: REJECT MODE COLLAPSE; ADAPT THE MATRIX.**

Keep exactly three Terminal modes:

- Practice.
- Scenario.
- Independent Assessment.

Define permitted assistance, Ghost behavior, Coach behavior, briefing, evidence, and entry navigation for each mode. Guided Practice and Independent Practice may be assistance policies within Practice, not replacements for the three modes.

The Scenario Bank remains the case library, and the case executes in Terminal. A neutral factual Case Briefing is permitted in all modes if it only states assigned work, authorized starting facts, constraints, and expected deliverable. It must not reveal hidden discovery facts, command sequences, or solutions. Coach instruction is not allowed during protected Independent Assessment.

Fable may refine navigation and assessment entry points, but should not silently narrow the learner's mental model or hide the distinction between a case and an execution mode.

### 4.10 R-10 — Defer diagnostic route

**Disposition: DEFER FORMAL DIAGNOSTIC; OPTIONAL SELF-DECLARED STARTING POINT MAY BE EVALUATED.**

A formal placement diagnostic needs a competency map, validated items, scoring, and routing rules. None should be invented prematurely. A self-declared choice such as “new to Amadeus” / “used Amadeus before” may change the first recommended lesson only; it has no score, evidence, or competence meaning. Fable can recommend whether even this small choice is needed.

### 4.11 R-11 — Defer five small items

**Disposition: SPLIT BY ITEM.**

- **R-11a cinematic transition duration:** defer to World/visual experience testing. Do not turn the tentative 180–260 ms suggestion into a current requirement.
- **R-11b cloned Coach voice:** future exploration only; no current build dependency.
- **R-11c speed/time-to-correct-work:** if useful, an elapsed-time event may be recorded silently for later research, but do not create a speed score, timer pressure, or job-readiness threshold before task validity and timing fairness are studied.
- **R-11d rule-defect impact re-evaluation:** retain as an essential rule-quality capability. A bounded internal script/report may be a future implementation choice, but full impact-scope re-evaluation after a confirmed defect is not optional.
- **R-11e prerequisite eligibility rechecks:** simplify triggers if possible, but preserve authoritative recomputation at the start boundary and the rule that delay alone is not negative evidence.

### 4.12 R-12 — Minimum Viable Coach by mode

**Disposition: ADAPT; DELEGATE DETAILED COACH DESIGN TO FABLE.**

Retain the role boundaries:

- Coach is a DEIXEN instructional/professional guide, not the customer.
- Coach is currently deterministic/rule-based in the core.
- Coach support is contextual and mode-bound.
- Coach cannot perform the learner's task or provide prohibited hints during Independent Assessment.
- Ghost demonstrates through the same underlying deterministic operational engine and creates no learner evidence.

Fable should define the smallest coherent Coach behavior for each mode and the boundary between factual Case Briefing, instructional Coach support, and post-task review. Do not create a broad conversational persona or LLM dependency for the Core.

## 8. Sonnet Competitive Positioning Review — Consolidation Disposition

The second Sonnet report is a separate market/competitive assignment. Its value is strategic framing and research questions, not a replacement for the first audit or proof of market conditions.

### 8.1 Retain as a positioning hypothesis

Keep this candidate idea for testing:

**“Learn it plainly. Do the whole job. See what you did alone.”**

It reflects the intended integration of:

- beginner-first teaching;
- a customer situation, discovery and bounded advice/choice;
- source-grounded Amadeus execution;
- task correctness and recovery;
- honest distinction between assisted and independent performance;
- clear evidence of what was demonstrated and under what conditions.

Do not market this as a proven competitive advantage or say no competitor offers the full journey. The report found no public evidence of a competitor demonstrating the whole chain, but it did not hands-on test every product.

### 8.2 Reframe the four original problems without overstating evidence

| Founder problem | Consolidation treatment |
|---|---|
| Expensive/inaccessible Amadeus learning | **Refine the claim:** better value for money and affordable work-shaped practice may be a goal. Do not say the whole market is expensive or that DEIXEN will be the cheapest; free/low-cost alternatives exist. |
| Knowing commands is not the same as doing the job | **Retain as central problem thesis**, supported by the report's competitor and practitioner observations, though local-market prevalence still needs validation. |
| Insufficient practical preparation and lower job competitiveness | **Retain as central problem thesis**, but test outcomes and avoid employment promises or claiming simulation equals work experience. |
| Superficial progress/assessment | **Retain as a core design problem**, with evidence validity and independence central to DEIXEN's product logic. |

The additional problems in the owner-direction record (not knowing where to start, unclear explanations, safe error practice, identifying weaknesses, transfer, workplace English, continued learning, and provider/employer visibility) remain candidates to validate. Do not convert every problem into a feature.

### 8.3 Competitive observations to retain with confidence labels

- GDS HUB, OTT/Viasinc, Open Study College, Exodus, IATA, and related providers are relevant alternatives in the wider learning/credential landscape.
- Free or low-cost options make a price-only strategy weak.
- The public-facing presence of lessons, emulators, quizzes, exams, certificates, and progress features means these are table stakes rather than unique features individually.
- The potential white space is at the integrated whole-task and trustworthy-evidence level, but this is a hypothesis, not a confirmed absence of competitor capability.

Always retain the report's limitations: no hands-on competitor testing; directory prices rather than systematic provider price verification; approximately twenty job advertisements as a convenience sample; thin Oman coverage; insufficient Egypt research; secondary sources for Saudi localization claims; and no evidence of employer acceptance or DEIXEN willingness to pay.

### 8.4 Commercial route: challenge the founder's hypothesis, do not replace it

Sonnet suggests that training providers may be an earlier commercial conversation than individual learners and that employers may be evidence validators before becoming buyers. Treat this as a useful challenge to the founder's original individual-first hypothesis, not as a decision.

Fable should compare:

- individual learner direct-to-consumer;
- training-provider / cohort route;
- employer-supported learning or evidence validation;
- combined routes.

For each, assess who uses, who pays, who decides, sales cycle, trust requirement, cost to serve, and evidence needed. The initial commercial buyer and market order remain open.

### 8.5 Three new professional-evidence capabilities

The founder has endorsed their strategic importance for evaluation and development, as captured in owner-direction Section 1.11. Preserve this status accurately.

#### N1 — Evidence Summary / potential Professional Evidence Profile

- A learner-facing, generated summary of demonstrated performance based on underlying attempt/evidence records.
- Starts naturally within Growth; may later develop into a learner-controlled professional portfolio/share view.
- Potentially includes tasks, competencies, case/version, conditions, result, independence/assistance, help requested/delivered, errors/recovery, meaningful variants, and evidence limits.
- Private by default; sharing is voluntary and scoped by the learner.
- Must distinguish DEIXEN-recorded simulated performance, practitioner-reviewed performance, and external certification/accreditation.
- Must never claim actual employment experience, job readiness, employer acceptance, or guaranteed hiring.
- Employer portal, talent directory, hiring marketplace, or live company progress access is **not approved** and requires demonstrated demand, privacy/security design, and founder approval.

#### N2 — Case & Provenance Validator

- Internal content/authoring quality-control capability.
- Checks required references, metadata, versioning, verification status, preconditions, expected outputs, links between cases/rules/sources, and detectable inconsistencies.
- Does not prove a source is authoritative, an interpretation is correct, or Amadeus behavior is true merely because a citation exists.
- Does not replace source verification or practitioner review.

#### N3 — Practitioner Review Export

- Internal export of a selected case, criteria/rubric, relevant attempt/event trace, outputs, assistance, error/recovery history, relevant source/provenance information, and blank practitioner review form.
- Should include context and chronology necessary to review the stated question, with data minimization, consent, and redaction safeguards.
- Export creation is not itself a review, approval, certification, or validation.
- Not a trainer dashboard, HR suite, appeal committee, or institutional administration workflow.

#### Relationship and recommended order

These are complementary, not an automatic sequential pipeline:

- N2 helps the team check content/source traceability.
- N3 packages selected material for actual independent professional review.
- N1 presents an appropriately bounded summary of the learner evidence that exists.

The shared foundation is a trustworthy, versioned evidence model. Fable should specify that model first, then recommend the smallest viable scope and sequence for the three capabilities. No assumption that all three ship together in the first Core.

### 8.6 Sonnet's “three-task job-shaped loop”

**Disposition: ADAPT.** Treat the suggested new reservation, retrieve-and-change, and error-recovery tasks as a candidate validation/vertical-slice plan, not as an automatic replacement for the founder's complete-local-Core requirement.

Ticketing, reissue, refunds, queues, irregular operations, and other work should be prioritized using EV-03 and market/practitioner evidence. Do not assume the first slice must include all of them, and do not assume a single reservation proves job-entry preparedness.

Retain as future/research candidates, not current requirements: provider cohort view after a provider commitment; bounded LLM expression after deterministic behavior is reliable; employer onboarding packs only with a partner; voice/World-lite/World/multi-customer shifts only when learning value is demonstrated; additional GDS only with market demand and practitioner coverage; continued development for working agents after content depth and update processes exist.

## 9. Evidence Gates and Required Validation Plan

All six evidence gates remain open. A report, a well-designed form, or a proposed tool does not close a gate.

| **EV-04 — Beginner usability / comprehension** | Whether genuine beginners understand instructions, can find next actions, use the dialogue, and distinguish help/output/evidence. | Initial exploratory sessions with the two available beginners; record task observation, comprehension, external help, confusion, and friction. - EV-01 and EV-02 are separate. A practitioner cannot make an unsupported Amadeus response true; a reference does not prove that the workflow is professionally appropriate.
- EV-03 is not closed by a single task or a book table of contents.
- EV-04 with two beginners is exploratory formative validation, not statistically generalizable learning-effect evidence.
- EV-05 requires meaningful variant differences and comparability, not merely changed names/dates.
- EV-06 cannot be inferred from public competitor marketing or a small set of job ads.
