## 0. Reading guide

### 0.1 How statements are marked

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[O]** | Observation: something I read in the package, in a founder clarification, or on a cited external page. |
| **[I]** | Interpretation: my reasoning from observations. It can be wrong. |
| **[H]** | Hypothesis: a testable claim that nobody has validated yet. |
| **[R]** | Recommendation: what I would do, and why. It is advice, not a decision. |

### 0.2 Evidence confidence labels (used for market claims)

- **Strong evidence**: confirmed on an official or primary page, or by several independent sources.
- **Moderate evidence**: confirmed by credible secondary sources or a small number of primary pages.
- **Limited evidence**: one source, a marketing claim, a directory listing, or a small convenience sample.
- **Unverified hypothesis**: my inference, with no direct source.

## 1. Executive Verdict — Is DEIXEN worth building, and why?

### 1.1 Verdict

> **Worth pursuing, but differentiation is not yet sufficiently established.**

I am keeping this verdict category after the founder's clarifications, and I want to be precise about why.

**The strategy is sound.** The founder's principle is to use familiar capabilities, deliver them through one coherent system, and add new features only when a genuine gap blocks the outcome. It matches where the evidence points. The market is full of single capabilities: lessons, Amadeus-style terminals, quizzes, exams, and AI customer role-play. I found no public product that evidences the whole chain from beginner-first teaching through customer situation, verified operational work, recovery, task-level validation and independence-labelled evidence. In that chain lies a plausible position.

**What is unproven is the outcome.** The founder's own standard is that a combination only becomes an advantage when it produces a better experience and result. Today that has not been shown, and the central claim depends on three things DEIXEN does not yet have:

1. **verified operational truth** (EV-01);
2. **practitioner-validated tasks and criteria** (EV-02/EV-03);
3. **someone outside the learner who will read and value the evidence** (EV-06).

A beginner comparing DEIXEN today with GDS HUB (free), an Online Travel Training / Viasinc course (listed at £329 for 90 hours with an emulator and certificate), or the IATA diploma (the credential a Riyadh employer's September 2026 posting makes mandatory) would not see the difference in a few seconds. The difference becomes visible only if the integration is built, taught and proven well.

### 1.2 The strategy, restated and tested

| Founder principle | My assessment |
|---|---|
| Differentiate through *how* familiar capabilities are designed, taught, integrated and delivered. | **Sound.** It is also the only credible route: the capabilities themselves are commodity. |
| Create new features only for genuine gaps. | **Sound, and under-applied in the package** (Stages 107–155 and the World/voice ideas are the main departures). |
| A combination is not an advantage until its value is shown in experience and outcomes. | **Correct, and it is why my verdict stays cautious.** |
| Beginner-first teaching ("the explanation must not require another explanation"). | **Strong candidate for a visible signature**, but it is also the founder's *only first-hand evidence of a current failure* (his early test). Execution quality is the risk. |
| Trustworthy, independence-aware assessment is a top priority. | **The most differentiating and the hardest to make credible.** Credibility is mostly external (validation, recognition), not internal machinery. |

### 1.3 What is potentially distinctive, and what is table stakes

**Potentially distinctive (all unproven):**

1. **[I]** The **whole-job unit**: a Professional Work Task in which customer discovery and authorization sit *inside* the task and "accepted but professionally wrong" is a first-class outcome.
2. **[I]** **Independence-labelled evidence**: assisted and independent performance recorded separately, with conditions. I found no public competitor that states this.
3. **[I]** **Verified, provenance-traced Amadeus behaviour**, valuable only if EV-01 is closed.
4. **[I]** **Beginner-first, English-first learning with in-product Arabic clarification**, tied to real tasks. I found no public product combining these for GDS work (Limited evidence; absence of public information is not proof).

**Table stakes (keep and execute exceptionally well, but do not market as differences):** structured lessons, demonstrations, a command terminal, guided practice, quizzes and checks, progress views, a rule-based coach. These are needed for completeness. Competitors all have versions of them.

**Not differentiators by themselves, and costly:** 3D worlds, cinematic scenes, voice, LLM-driven customers, multi-customer shifts. The package itself says so (Objectives §6).

### 1.5 What would change this verdict

**Toward "strongly worth pursuing":**
- A practitioner confirms the engine's behaviour in scope (EV-01/EV-02).
- Blinded practitioners score DEIXEN attempts consistently.
- Several training providers or employers say they would act on the evidence.
- A pilot shows DEIXEN-trained beginners doing better on an unseen work sample than beginners from a course alone (Appendix A).
- Beginners rate explanations as understandable *without* extra help (the founder's own test, made measurable).

**Toward "not sufficiently differentiated":**
- Practitioners find Amadeus behaviour cannot be reproduced faithfully even within a small scope.
- Employers and providers say they screen only by IATA certificate and experience and would not look at simulation evidence.
- Amadeus's partner programme or licence terms make an "Amadeus-style" product untenable.
- The customer interaction feels like a multiple-choice quiz to beginners.

### 1.6 If I were responsible: seven moves

1. **Put a working Amadeus practitioner on the project now**, as a paid reviewer, not at Stage 2.
2. **Test the customer interaction with a human first** (Wizard-of-Oz, Appendix A). Three to five beginners will show whether the mechanism works.
3. **Shrink the first build to a "job-shaped loop"**: new reservation with discovery and authorization, retrieve-and-change, and one error-recovery case, with a *simple* evidence record and a readable evidence summary.
4. **Make beginner-first teaching a measurable release gate**: no learner-facing text ships until beginners can follow it without outside help (§5.4).
5. **Start ten customer-discovery conversations now** (training providers, employers, recent entry-level hires), in parallel with the build.
6. **Freeze the proposal register** and convert it into a short build specification. Five beginners will teach more than another 155 stages.
7. **Investigate Amadeus's Learning Partners programme and the legal posture on "Amadeus" naming early.** They affect both the moat question and the partnership path.

## 2. What DEIXEN Actually Is — synthesized understanding

### 2.1 In plain words

**[I]** DEIXEN is trying to be the place where a complete beginner (possibly a beginner in both Amadeus and English) does the *job* of a travel-operations agent, not just the course. A learner meets a customer request, works out what is missing, advises and gets authorization, performs the work in a stateful Amadeus-style terminal, checks that the result really satisfies the request, recovers from problems, and closes the interaction. Each attempt leaves a record of what was asked, under what conditions, what help was used, what went wrong, and whether the learner did it alone. The founder wants that record to stand in for the thing beginners lack: *experience*.

Underneath, the product is two assets inside a delivery system:

1. a **library of professionally validated work tasks** (cases and variants);
2. an **engine and evidence model** that can truthfully say what a learner demonstrated.

Learning, Ghost, Coach, Home and Growth deliver those assets in a way a beginner can follow.

### 2.2 Status map: what is established and what is not

| Status | Items |
|---|---|
| **Current product direction** | Professional Work Task as the organizing unit. Five areas (Home, Learning, Terminal, Scenario Bank, Growth). Terminal with exactly three modes (Practice, Scenario, Independent Assessment). Scenario Bank as a case library whose cases run in the Terminal. Deterministic, rules-based Coach and Ghost. Local single-user core first. Backend later, not cancelled. |
| **Founder-approved principles** | No invention of Amadeus behaviour. Separate conversation, task and operational state. Separate correctness dimensions. Independent assessment is protected from Coach and Ghost rescue. Evidence is private by default, with corrections additive. No numeric readiness scores, employment guarantees or implied Amadeus affiliation. **Added by the clarifications:** beginner-first teaching; English as the primary learning and customer language with in-product Arabic clarification; feature discipline (new features only for genuine gaps); no claim that simulation equals workplace experience. |
| **Working proposals needing validation** | The first task (simple new reservation: one adult, one segment, one fare). Five-object runtime model. Customer simulation by authored deterministic text. Stages 107–155 assessment, exposure, hold and correction rules. Home vs Flight Deck naming. |
| **Future capabilities** | DEIXEN World / Virtual Company, cinematic scenes, voice, LLM-expressed customers, multi-customer shifts and Shift Evidence Records, other GDSs, the Backend. |
| **Not established** | Any validated learning effect. Employer acceptance. Willingness to pay. Partnership with Amadeus. Institutional recognition. Current application behaviour (repository audit pending). Curriculum beyond Slice 1. |

### 2.3 The five areas as one system, and the learner journey

**[I]** The five areas only work as one system through two things: the **Case Package** (what the learner is asked to do) and the **Attempt / evidence record** (what they did and how independently). The founder's clarification states the intended journey in ten steps: beginner-friendly learning; demonstration; guided practice; increasingly independent performance; a complete customer situation; verified operational environment; decisions, changes and recovery; evaluation of the operational *and* professional outcome; a record of what was demonstrated and under what conditions; a next development step from the evidence. I test each step against the market in §4.3.

**[I]** If the evidence record is weak or unreadable, the system collapses into five pages. That is why §9 treats the evidence record, and not any single screen, as the product's real output.

### 2.5 The problems DEIXEN exists to solve (as stated by the founder)

**Original problems (founder's second clarification):**

- **A.** Expensive access to Amadeus knowledge.
- **B.** The gap between knowing commands and performing real work.
- **C.** Insufficient practical preparation for employment.
- **D.** Superficial progress and assessment systems.

**Additional candidate problems (explicitly *not* established findings):** beginners not knowing where to start; explanations too difficult for beginners; no safe place to practise mistakes, changing requests and recovery; difficulty identifying specific weaknesses and the next step; success in rehearsed exercises but struggle with unfamiliar situations; limited chances to build workplace English through real tasks; products losing value after the course; providers and employers unable to see what a learner can actually perform.

§3.5 tests each one against external evidence, and §9 maps each to existing capabilities.

### 2.6 What the package gets right, in one paragraph

**[O/I]** The package is unusually honest about the difference between a proposal and a fact. It separates syntax, operational and task correctness. It refuses readiness percentages. It insists on provenance for Amadeus behaviour. It puts learner privacy and evidence integrity ahead of gamification. It sequences ambition (core before Backend before World). These are real strengths that most competitors visibly lack. The weaknesses are about **where the effort has gone**, **what is still unproven**, and **who will care**.

### 3.3 Regional picture

**United Arab Emirates [Limited to Moderate evidence].** Dubai and Sharjah classroom providers list Amadeus, Galileo and Sabre courses. Directory prices I saw ranged from about AED 1,500 to AED 4,500, plus a Sharjah institute listed at USD 2,950 and a shorter electronic-booking-tools course listed at USD 477 with a KHDA accreditation mention. These are directory listings, not provider quotes. Job postings in my sample ask for one to two years' experience for ticketing roles. One Abu Dhabi posting is for UAE nationals only. A Gulf News report from the post-pandemic recovery (undated in the excerpt, c. 2022) quotes an Abu Dhabi agency that hires freshers and trains them from scratch, and calls that training time-consuming. That is an employer pain point, but a single dated quote.

**Saudi Arabia [Moderate evidence, secondary sources].**
- *Regulation.* Saudi Arabia's tourism Saudization reserves some roles entirely for nationals and sets quotas for others. Phase one took effect on 22 April 2026. Press summaries put travel-agent and travel-ticket-clerk roles in the 50% group and managerial and agency-director roles in the exclusive group. I did not read the HRSD primary texts.
- *Funding.* HRDF (HADAF) runs employment-linked programmes: Tamheer (3–6 months of on-the-job training for Saudi graduates with a monthly reward), wage subsidies of up to 50% for tourism roles, and a published list of HRDF-approved training institutes among eligible entities. Trade press reports large tourism training agreements. These routes pay for *outcomes* and favour Saudi nationals.
- *Education.* The TVTC Travel and Tourism diploma curriculum (dated 1439H, about 2018) includes automated reservation systems and ticketing. A King Faisal University applied diploma course covers Amadeus and Galileo reservations and pricing. Travelport has a published MoU with Al Yamamah University. IATA courses are offered locally (for example by Blue Ocean Academy in Jeddah and an IATA partner in Riyadh).
- *Implication.* **[I]** The funded Saudi demand is mainly for Saudi nationals, delivered through institutions. The Saudi user and the Saudi buyer are therefore different people, and the buyer may be a provider, employer or fund.

**Oman [Limited evidence].** I found one 2022 report of a Sultan Qaboos University and Oman Air programme that included familiarization with ticket booking, and one air-ticketing posting whose pay is quoted in omani rials (RO 200–350; the location is not shown in the excerpt). I found no credible Omani provider evidence. **I draw no market conclusion for Oman.**

**Egypt (pilot context) [Limited evidence].** Arabic free courses (m3aarf, Udemy Arabic) are widespread. One Cairo training centre advertises two-week courses on a "live" Amadeus with certificates from Amadeus Egypt and IATA. That is a forum-level marketing claim and unverified. Egypt matters here mainly as the founder's pilot environment, not as a target employment market.

### 4.6 Where the white space is, and what could close it

**[I]** The public market is dense in lessons, terminals and exams, and thin in (a) customer-in-the-loop operational tasks, (b) task-level validation of professional correctness, (c) independence-labelled evidence, and (d) beginner-first bilingual scaffolding for GDS work.

**[H] Ways the white space could close:**
1. A free simulator (for example GDS HUB) adds scenario grading. It is cheap to attempt, but fidelity and validation would still need practitioners.
2. An AI role-play vendor adds a GDS emulator. Strong on conversation, weak on operational truth.
3. Amadeus extends its Learning Partners offer with scenario assessment. This is the most serious, because it owns the system and the credential.
4. A large provider such as an OTT/Viasinc reseller adds practical assessments to its existing certificates.

**[I]** None of these is imminent on the public evidence, but none is difficult in principle. DEIXEN's protection is therefore the *validated content library, the credibility of the evidence, and the quality of the beginner experience*, and each must be earned (§5.3).

## 5. DEIXEN's Potential Differentiators

### 5.1 Is DEIXEN actually different?

**[I] Short answer.** Not by features. Possibly by the *experience and outcome* those features produce when they are connected, taught and evidenced well. The founder's principle is the right test, and the journey test (§4.3) shows the chain from step 4 to step 9 is where public evidence runs out. A larger platform of familiar parts would not be different. A **verified, beginner-first, whole-job practice system that records what the learner did alone** would be, if it works.

**[I] Honest caution.** The selection literature does not say work-sample evidence beats knowledge tests. Sackett et al. It is "performance evidence is *different information* that a certificate cannot provide, and it is what the experience-based hiring filter actually asks for". That must be tested, not asserted.

### 5.4 Beginner-first teaching as a recognizable part of DEIXEN

**What competitors provide.** Arabic-medium content is common and often free (m3aarf, Udemy Arabic, GDS HUB's Arabic pages). Paid English-medium courses (OTT, Open Study College, IATA) assume the learner can follow English instruction. I found **no public product that combines plain-English-first learning with in-product Arabic clarification keyed to GDS tasks**, but I could not test any product's explanation quality (**Limited evidence; RFI**).

**What DEIXEN has.** The package describes bilingual lesson material and Coach explanations of system output. I did not verify their quality. The founder's early test is the only first-hand evidence, and it says **the explanations currently require further explanation**.

**Why it could become part of the identity.** It is visible within seconds, it answers problems E2 and E6, and it matches how entry-level postings read (English demanded almost everywhere, "basic English" accepted at entry).

**Risks.**
- **Double load**: a beginner in both Amadeus and English can fail from either side.
- **Translation crutch**: unlimited Arabic help can slow English development, which is itself a stated outcome.
- **Terminology drift** between lessons, Coach and customer dialogue.
- **Accuracy loss** when simplifying a professional concept.
- It is a **quality advantage**, not a structural one: anyone who invests in writing and testing can match it.

**[R] Classification: Improve or refine** (existing Learning, Coach and bilingual material), with a **small enhancement** (one shared glossary reused across Learning, Coach, case briefs and customer dialogue). It is not a new feature area.

**[R] A minimum standard that makes "the explanation must not require another explanation" testable:**
1. **Explain before use.** A term appears in a task only after it has been introduced with a plain explanation and an example.
2. **Controlled vocabulary per level**, with one glossary as the single source of truth.
3. **System output explained step by step** at beginner levels, never only the raw response.
4. **Arabic clarification on demand**, for explanations, glossary terms and customer messages, not as a default translation of everything.
5. **Language support recorded as a condition of the attempt.** It should be neither blocked nor hidden. For a job done in English, independence in English is part of what is being evidenced. **This is a decision for the founder:** whether Arabic clarification counts as assistance in Independent Assessment.
6. **A practitioner correctness check** on every simplified explanation (simple must not mean wrong).
7. **A release gate:** no learner-facing explanation ships until a small group of beginners who have not seen it can follow it without outside help. The team sets the threshold; I do not invent one.

### 5.7 Where the founder's thinking is strong, weak, complicated or not ambitious enough

**Strong.** The unit of learning is the whole job. Evidence is honest about conditions. Truth discipline is explicit. Readiness percentages and employment guarantees are refused. The sequence protects the core. The clarifications add beginner-first teaching and feature discipline, both sound.

**Weak or unproven assumptions.**
- That employers or providers will value the evidence (EV-06 is open).
- That a deterministic customer can feel real to a beginner.
- That a simulated engine can be verified to a standard practitioners accept (EV-01).
- That "expensive access" is the main problem (§3.5).
- That trainees are the right first customer (§8).

**Unnecessarily complicated (for now).** The assessment-integrity rule set designed before any beginner has used the product; the World, voice and cinematic ideas; a very large register of proposals (155 provisional stages, about half of them for out-of-scope workflows or deferred machinery).

**Not ambitious enough.** The first task is narrow (one adult, one segment, one fare) against job postings that name servicing, pricing, ticketing, reissue and refund. There is no plan to reach employers or providers early. The evidence has no outward-facing form. There is no deliberate capture of beginner error patterns, which could become the product's most defensible data.

**What I would do differently.** Put a practitioner on the team now, test the customer interaction with a human, shrink the first build to a three-task job-shaped loop with a readable evidence summary, hold ten discovery conversations in parallel, and make beginner comprehension a release gate.

## 7. The Strongest Competitive Positioning

Everything in this section is a **positioning hypothesis** to be tested in discovery conversations (§8, Appendix B). It is led by the problems the evidence supports best (B, C, D, E2, E6) and not by features.

### 7.1 Category, and what to avoid being perceived as

**[R] Category:** *work-readiness practice and evidence for travel reservations*, in plain terms a **job simulation for reservations agents**. It should sit beside courses and credentials, not inside them.

**Avoid being perceived as:**
- *an Amadeus course or another LMS* (a crowded, low-priced category);
- *a command simulator* (GDS HUB is free and broad);
- *an AI travel-agent chatbot* (a different problem);
- *a certificate or a job guarantee* (the package already refuses this, and employers name IATA, not DEIXEN);
- *a game* (it dilutes the professional claim).

### 7.2 The strongest value proposition

**[R]** Learn Amadeus from zero, in plain English with Arabic help. Practise the whole agent job with real customer situations. See exactly what you can do on your own. Practice and evidence, not a certificate and not a job guarantee.

### 7.3 What a beginner should understand in a few seconds

Three short ideas: **plain English, Arabic help**; **real customer cases, not just commands**; **a record of what you did alone**. Anything longer means the positioning is not yet clear.

### 7.4 What should distinguish it from a course or a simulator

| A course | A simulator | DEIXEN (if the claim is earned) |
|---|---|---|
| Tests whether you remember | Tests whether you can enter commands | Tests whether you can complete the customer's job correctly, and shows what you did without help |

**[I]** The claim must be earned by the product and by external validation. A single line on a landing page proves nothing.

### 7.5 What would make an existing professional keep using it

**[H]** Cases that a live system cannot offer safely: irregular operations, rare changes, mistakes that would cost money in production. This has no evidence yet. It needs interviews with working agents (Appendix B). I keep it secondary (E7).

### 7.6 What could interest a training provider or an employer

**[H]** *Providers:* a practice and assessment layer that sits beside their IATA or Amadeus courses, with evidence of what each student can perform. *Employers:* readable evidence that shortens onboarding. A UAE agency said training freshers is time-consuming, but that is one dated quote. Both audiences also need to understand what the evidence does **not** prove.

### 7.7 Relationship to the IATA credential

**[R]** Position DEIXEN as a **complement to** IATA and Amadeus certificates, not a replacement. The credential is what postings name. The assessment gap in that credential (multiple-choice, closed book) is the legitimate opening. Do not claim equivalence, acceptance or preparation for a specific exam without checking.

### 7.8 Claims discipline

Do not say or imply: "certified", "job-ready", "employers accept this", "real Amadeus", a readiness percentage, or that simulated work equals workplace experience. The founder's clarification already requires this. I repeat it because the pressure to overclaim is strongest at launch.

## 8. Commercial and Customer Implications

Commercial attractiveness is a separate question from educational distinctiveness, and I evaluate it separately. I did not find pricing, market size or willingness-to-pay evidence for DEIXEN, and I do not invent any.

### 8.1 Who uses the product, and who pays

| Role | Uses DEIXEN? | Pays? | What they want |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Individual learner** | Yes | Possibly, but price-sensitive and surrounded by free alternatives | A route to a first job; understandable teaching |
| **Training provider or institute** | Yes (with learners) | Yes, if outcomes improve | Placement results, less instructor time, evidence per student |
| **Employer or agency** | Receives evidence; may use for onboarding | Possibly | Faster onboarding, readable evidence |
| **Funder** (for example employment-linked programmes in Saudi Arabia) | No | Pays providers for outcomes | Verified outcomes |
| **Working professional** | Maybe | Possibly | Practice on hard cases |

**[I]** User and payer are very likely **different people**. That matters for product design (the package defers cohort and trainer tooling) and for the order in which to sell.

### 8.2 Is "trainees, then employees, then companies" the right sequence?

**[I]** It is a sensible **product-maturity** sequence. As a **market-entry** sequence it has weaknesses:
1. Trainees have the lowest purchasing power and the most free substitutes.
2. Their evidence needs *someone else's* acceptance to matter, and the people who give acceptance are employers.
3. Providers already own distribution to beginners and a budget, and they care about placement.
4. Employers hold the signal DEIXEN most needs to learn about.

**[R] A refinement for the founder to consider:**
- **Pilot** with trusted beginners now, for product validation (already planned).
- Make the **first commercial conversation** with training providers and vocational programmes, as design partners.
- Use **employers as acceptance validators** before treating them as buyers.
- Treat **working professionals** as a later segment.

**[H]** This assumes providers will pay and employers will read evidence. Both are untested, so the first act is to ask them (Appendix B).

### 8.3 Why an educationally distinctive product can be commercially hard

1. Buyers buy credentials and jobs, not teaching methods.
2. Free substitutes set the price anchor for basic content.
3. A new kind of evidence has a trust deficit that only third parties can remove.
4. Each validated task is expensive, while the price per learner is low.
5. The system's owner (Amadeus) can offer its own environment and certification to institutions.

### 8.4 Candidate initial segments (all hypotheses, ordered by what the evidence supports)

| Segment | Why it might work | Evidence | What would disprove it |
|---|---|---|---|

GDS-specific beginner roles are few in my sample; many are hotel programmes. | Eligibility rules or roles do not match; the funder wants a different credential |

| **Employers and agencies** (as validators first) | Onboarding pain; evidence gap | Limited (one dated quote; postings show screening by experience) | They say they would not read simulation evidence |

Each needs interviews or pilot results, not desk research.

### 9.2 The only new capabilities that pass the test

Applying the seven-step decision sequence to every gap, only **three small items** remain. None is a new product area.

**N1. Learner-controlled shareable Evidence Summary.** *Classification: Add only if necessary (small).*
- **Unmet need:** providers and employers cannot see what a learner can perform (E8), and private evidence cannot help the learner be considered for jobs.
- **User and benefit:** the learner chooses to share a plain summary; the reader sees task, conditions, assistance, errors, outcome and validator version.
- **Why current capabilities are insufficient:** the evidence record exists, but no one else can read it.
- **Competitor evidence:** certificates of completion exist everywhere (PC). None I found records conditions or assistance (NPE).
- **Minimum implementation:** one read-only page or PDF generated on demand, with a plain statement of what it does **not** prove. No verification service, no Backend.
- **Stage:** the on-screen summary belongs in the local core; the shareable form comes after internal testing and employer feedback.
- **Cost, dependencies, risks:** low to medium; depends on the evidence schema (G5) and external feedback; the risk is misuse as a hiring proof or an implied guarantee.
- **How to test:** show summaries to five to eight practitioners, providers or employers. Ask whether it tells them more than a certificate, and what they would still want.

**N2. Case and variant schema validator with provenance checks (internal tool).** *Classification: Add only if necessary (internal).*
- **Unmet need:** keeping every behaviour traceable to a verified source as the library grows (G1, G11).
- **Why existing capabilities are insufficient:** provenance discipline is a principle, but without an automated check it relies on memory.
- **Minimum implementation:** a script or test that fails when a case or response lacks a source reference or verification status.
- **Stage:** local core. **Cost:** small. **Risk:** over-building into an authoring product. **Test:** it catches deliberately seeded gaps.

**N3. Practitioner review export (internal tool).** *Classification: Add only if necessary (internal).*
- **Unmet need:** letting an external practitioner review and score recorded attempts without touching the app (G1, §9.7).
- **Minimum implementation:** export of an attempt's events and results in a readable file, plus a blank scoring sheet.
- **Stage:** internal testing. **Cost:** small. **Risk:** low. **Test:** two practitioners can score the same attempt and the team can compare their scores.

**Considered and not recommended as new features:** dashboards, additional scenario volume, AI features, 3D or cinematic presentation, and further assessment machinery. The first four needs those would serve (A to D) are met by the improvements above.

### 9.3 What the core must contain to be complete enough for genuine internal testing

**[R]** The smallest coherent product that delivers the intended value, with a **job-shaped loop** as its scope:

1. **A beginner-first learning path** for the supported tasks, meeting the standard in §5.4.
2. **Ghost demonstrations** of those tasks through the same engine.
3. **The Terminal with three modes** and a **verified command and behaviour scope**, listed explicitly.
4. **A small Scenario Bank:** roughly three task types (new reservation with discovery and authorization; retrieve and change; one error-recovery case), each with at least two variants, including one *changed-condition* variant for transfer.
5. **A customer interaction mechanism** chosen after the human test (G2).
6. **A task-level validator** for those cases, specified with a practitioner.
7. **Independence conditions, an attempt and evidence record, and a readable Evidence Summary**, with the minimum integrity rules in §6.2.
8. **Growth** as a view over the evidence, with simple next-step rules.
9. **Language support** (English-first, Arabic on demand) with a single glossary, and a decision on whether it is a recorded condition (G5).
10. **Enough instrumentation** to learn from pilots: an event log, plus an export for review.

### 9.4 What to deliberately exclude from the first core build

Full exposure tracking, disclosure holds and correction propagation; start-race handling; World, voice, cinematic scenes and LLM customers; multi-customer shifts; trainer, instructor and institutional workflows; additional GDSs; ticketing, reissue and refund depth (a **roadmap item right after validation**, not far-future); dashboards beyond the evidence view; and any numerical readiness figure.

### 9.5 Decisions to resolve, and details that can stay open

| Decision | Why it matters | Needed by |
|---|---|---|
| The verified command and behaviour scope for the first release, and its source of truth (**EV-01**) | Everything else depends on it | Before implementation of the engine scope |
| The customer interaction mechanism | Determines the whole feel of "integrated" | After the human test, before building |
| The case set and the changed-condition variants | Defines transfer testing | Before internal testing |
| The task validator specification (**with a practitioner**) | Defines professional correctness | During core build |

**Can stay open without blocking development:** Home versus Flight Deck naming, visual identity and logo details, Coach presentation style, World design, voice choices, the Backend technology, pricing, and institution strategy.

### 9.6 Architectural qualities to preserve (strategic level)

Keep the **operational engine** separate from the user interface. Keep **content** (cases, variants, rule sets, glossary) as versioned data. Record attempts as an **append-only event log** from which evidence is derived. Put persistence behind a **replaceable boundary** so a Backend can replace local storage without rewriting the engine. Give every verified behaviour a **source and status field**. Keep the user identity abstract so accounts can be added later. Make **export and import** possible from day one. Do not design the Backend now. **These are qualities, not a design; investigate the repository before deciding anything.**

### 9.7 What to test, and with whom

**Technical testing before the internal pilot:**
- golden transcripts for every verified interaction, replayed as regression tests;
- state-transition tests, including "accepted by the terminal but professionally wrong" cases;
- mode isolation: Ghost and Coach cannot leak help or evidence into Independent Assessment;
- attempt start, resume and reload behave consistently;
- provenance completeness (the N2 check);
- glossary consistency: every term used is defined before first use;
- Arabic and English rendering, including right-to-left text around command lines;
- basic accessibility and performance on modest devices;
- export works and is readable.

**Founder and trusted beginners:**
- Start with the **Wizard-of-Oz customer test**.
- Observe about five true beginners (no prior GDS) with think-aloud sessions: time to first successful task, unprompted comprehension, help sought outside the product, use of the Arabic aid, and performance on changed-condition variants. This is qualitative usability work; it does not establish effectiveness.
- **Founder's own journey:** treat him as a **builder-learner case study**. Pre-register the tasks and conditions, log all outside help, have an external practitioner score the attempts, and take a baseline before he begins. Do not present his result as "from zero to job" evidence.
- Ask each beginner to explain, in their own words, what the evidence summary says about them.

**Independent Amadeus practitioner (cannot be replaced by founder or beginner testing):**
- verify command behaviour, state changes, warnings and recovery within the supported scope (**EV-01**);
- review the realism and professional correctness of the cases and the customer dialogue (**EV-02**);
- contribute a **job-task analysis** from practitioners in at least two target markets, to check scope against real work (**EV-03**);
- score recorded attempts independently, so agreement with the validator can be measured;
- check that simplified explanations remain accurate.

### 9.8 What must be added or changed before public launch

The Backend and production protections; security and privacy review under the data-protection rules of each target market (**not researched here**); accessibility and language quality assurance in English and Arabic; a legal review of product claims, trademarks and the Amadeus relationship; payment and terms; support; a process for re-verifying behaviour when Amadeus changes; provider- and employer-facing materials tested with real readers; and real-world usability testing in each target market.

### 9.9 Adding World and LLM later without compromising integrity

**Principles for any LLM use:**
- The LLM may **express** customer language within a case's fixed facts. It must not decide operational state, validation or scoring.
- Where it interprets learner input, it should **map text to predefined intents** with a deterministic fallback, and the evidence should log both the raw text and the mapped intent.
- Case facts are tested adversarially so the customer cannot contradict the case.
- The same case must be assessable the same way with or without the LLM.
- Dedicated and integrated testing is required before any public use, as the founder's sequence already says.

**Principles for World:**
- World is a **presentation layer** reading task state. It must not own state.
- Test the value cheaply first with a **World-lite** experiment: one customer, text dialogue, a static scene. Proceed only if learners show better engagement or transfer and providers ask for it.
- Voice needs a text equivalent and the same evidence logging.

**Gate:** World and LLM begin only after the core passes internal and professional validation and the Backend decision is made. They are separate scopes with their own acceptance tests, not additions to the core.

### 11.4 Closing assessment

DEIXEN's strategy, as the founder has now clarified it, is the right kind: familiar capabilities made distinctive by how they connect, with new features only for real gaps. The evidence supports the problems at its centre (commands versus work, thin practical preparation, superficial assessment, difficulty for beginners, and workplace English). It does not yet support the claims about price, employer acceptance or learning effect, and the foundation of verified operational truth is open. The most valuable next steps are not more features: they are a practitioner, a human test of the customer interaction, a small job-shaped first build, a measurable beginner-first standard, and conversations with the providers and employers whose reactions will decide whether the evidence is worth anything outside the product.

## Appendix A — Proposed validation experiments (advisory)

| # | Experiment | Question | Method | Success signal (the team defines thresholds) | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Wizard-of-Oz customer test** | Can a beginner conduct discovery and authorization with a scripted customer, and does it feel like a real task? | A human plays the customer from the case script; observe three to five beginners | Beginners complete the task without confusion about the mechanism; they describe it as like a customer | Before the customer mechanism is built |
| A2 | **Beginner comprehension gate** | Can a beginner follow each explanation without outside help? | Beginners who have not seen the content work through it; record help sought outside the product | Few or no external lookups; correct unprompted explanations | Before each learning release |
| A3 | **Practitioner scoring agreement** | Do independent practitioners score attempts the way the validator does? | Two practitioners score recorded attempts blind to the validator | Agreement is high enough that disagreements can be explained | Internal testing |
| A4 | **Pilot comparison** | Do DEIXEN-trained beginners perform better on an unseen work sample than beginners from a course alone? | Small groups; the same unseen task; blind practitioner scoring. Informative, not conclusive, at small scale | A visible, explainable difference that survives scrutiny | After the core is stable |
| A5 | **Evidence-summary reading test** | Does the summary tell providers and employers something a certificate does not? | Show summaries to five to eight readers; ask what they learn and what is missing | Readers can describe what the learner did alone; they ask for it, or say what they would add | After internal testing |
| A6 | **World-lite experiment (later)** | Does a scene add learning value over text? | One customer, text dialogue, a static scene; compare engagement and transfer | Measurable benefit that justifies cost | Only if justified |

## Appendix B — Discovery conversation guide (advisory)

**Training providers and IATA/Amadeus course centres.** What do your graduates struggle with in their first job? How do you assess practical readiness today? What do you pay for practice tools? What would you need to see before using a new tool alongside your course? Who would approve it? What outcome data would you want?

**Employers and agencies.** What do you screen for in entry-level hires? How much of onboarding is spent on system versus customer handling? Would you read a one-page summary of what a candidate did without help? What would make it credible? What would make you ignore it?

**Recent entry-level hires.** What surprised you about the first month? Which tasks were hardest? What did you wish you had practised? How did you prove yourself before you had experience?

**Working agents.** Which cases do you rarely see but fear? What do you look up repeatedly? Would you practise on a safe environment, and what would bring you back?

**Practitioner reviewer.** Which behaviours in the supported scope are easiest to get wrong? Which professional mistakes does a system accept but a supervisor would not?

**Funders and programme managers.** What outcomes do you pay for? Who approves a tool? What evidence do you require?
